# Cascade Warehouse Club | FY2026 Audit Risk Assessment

**2026 Beta Alpha Psi Accounting & Finance Case Competition**  
**Washington State University | October 10, 2026**

## About this project

I participated in Washington State University's 2026 Beta Alpha Psi Accounting & Finance Case Competition as a Data Analytics student. Working with **Dorji Damdul and Isaac Hernandez**, I helped develop an audit risk assessment for **Cascade Warehouse Club, Inc.**, a fictional membership warehouse retailer.

Our challenge was to act as independent audit associates and decide **where the financial statements were most likely to be misstated, how significant those risks might be, and how to use a limited audit budget to investigate them**. We developed our analysis and presentation during the competition's 2.5-hour preparation window.

**This project is an audit-planning exercise, not a completed audit.** Our estimates identify potential exposures, not confirmed accounting errors.

## The case

Cascade operated 58 warehouses across the Pacific Northwest and Alaska. In FY2026, it reported approximately **$9.59 billion in revenue** and **$336 million in operating income**.

Several developments made its financial reporting more difficult to assess: a new inventory and ERP system at 35 warehouses, a supplier rebate agreement tied to annual purchasing targets, and a change in the estimated percentage of gift card balances that customers would never redeem. Management also had an incentive to reach **$330 million in operating income**, the threshold for executive bonuses.

Our team was asked to rank three risks, estimate possible misstatements, design audit procedures, evaluate an internal control, and explain where we would spend less audit time.

## Our key findings

| Priority audit risk | What we identified | Proposed audit response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Supplier rebate recognition** | Cascade accrued a **$22.0M** rebate at the 5% tier. Our baseline projection of **$644M** in calendar-year purchases fell below the **$650M** threshold. If only the 2% tier is supportable, the rebate receivable could be overstated by **$13.2M**, with a potential **$15.4M operating-income effect** after accounting for unsold inventory. | Confirm contract terms and purchases with the supplier, challenge purchase forecasts, and recalculate the rebate allocation between inventory and cost of goods sold. |
| **Inventory and ERP conversion** | Changes to inventory systems, self-checkout, cycle counts, and approval permissions increased recording and shrink risks. A targeted shrink sensitivity indicated approximately **$4.0M–$5.5M** of additional potential losses under our assumptions, separate from other ERP exposures. | Observe physical counts, trace manually recorded receipts, review inventory adjustments, and test ERP access and approval controls. |
| **Cash Card breakage** | Management increased its breakage assumption from **5% to 8%**, recognizing a **$6.4M** one-time revenue adjustment. Older card cohorts had only about **3.7%–4.1%** unredeemed after 36 months, raising questions about the new estimate. | Reperform redemption analysis using transaction data, test the journal entry, and reconcile the Cash Card liability. |

**Audit materiality in the case:** $16.0M overall; $12.0M performance materiality; $0.8M clearly trivial threshold.

### Important assumptions

These estimates are scenarios, not findings of fact:

- **$644M purchase forecast:** $440M through August, plus four months at the $55M monthly average, less $16M for the planned supplier change in November and December. Strong holiday purchases could still put Cascade above $650M.
- **$15.4M potential income effect:** If the lower rebate tier applies, 2% of $440M is $8.8M. Assuming 25% of the related merchandise is unsold, only $6.6M would reduce current-year cost of goods sold, versus the $22M recorded.
- **Inventory sensitivity:** We used separate assumptions for the older warehouses and the converted warehouses rather than applying a high self-checkout shrink rate across all annual sales.
- **Cash Cards:** A balance still unredeemed after 36 months is not necessarily the same thing as lifetime breakage. More evidence would be needed before proposing an adjustment.

## What I contributed

My primary focus was the **supplier rebate analysis**. I:

- Developed and explained the **$644M annual purchase projection** using case data and the planned supplier change.
- Evaluated the potential **$13.2M excess rebate accrual** and the related **$15.4M operating-income scenario**.
- Helped develop audit procedures to test rebate eligibility and its accounting treatment.
- Presented our supplier rebate findings to the judges.
- Explained why **property, plant, and equipment (PP&E)** could receive relatively less additional audit attention than higher-risk areas, while still requiring appropriate testing.

The risk assessment, conclusions, and original competition presentation were **team efforts**. This repository includes a **post-competition portfolio revision** of the slide deck rather than the exact version we presented that day.
