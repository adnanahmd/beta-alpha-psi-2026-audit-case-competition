# Cascade Warehouse Club | FY2026 Audit Risk Assessment

**2026 Beta Alpha Psi Accounting & Finance Case Competition**  
Washington State University · October 10, 2026

## Project overview

This repository documents a team audit-planning exercise from the 2026 Beta Alpha Psi case competition. Our team acted as audit associates assessing the FY2026 financial-reporting risks of **Cascade Warehouse Club, Inc.**, a fictional membership-warehouse retailer.

The assignment was to rank the three most significant risks of material misstatement; estimate their potential dollar exposure; recommend targeted audit procedures; evaluate an internal control; and justify where to allocate relatively less audit effort. **This was an audit plan, not an actual audit**, and the possible misstatements discussed below were not established errors.

## Case background

Cascade operated 58 warehouses and reported approximately **$9.592 billion in FY2026 revenue** and **$336 million in operating income**. It implemented a new inventory/ERP system at 35 warehouses, signed a volume-rebate agreement with Summit Foods, and changed its estimated Cash Card breakage rate. Reported operating income also exceeded a **$330 million executive bonus threshold**.

## Key findings

| Risk area | Evidence and scenario | Planned audit response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Supplier rebate recognition** | Cascade accrued **$22.0M** at the 5% tier. A simple **$644M** calendar-year purchase forecast falls below the **$650M** threshold. If only 2% is supportable, rebate receivables may be overstated by **$13.2M**, and operating income by **$15.4M** when the assumed inventory allocation is considered. | Independently confirm contract terms and purchases; scrutinize forecasts; recalculate the rebate and its allocation to inventory versus COGS. |
| **Inventory and ERP conversion** | A group-specific shrink sensitivity suggests approximately **$4.04M–$5.52M** of incremental loss, under stated assumptions, in addition to exposure from manual receipts and weaknesses in inventory-adjustment authorization. | Observe counts, reconcile cycle-count results and manual receipts, inspect system permissions and adjustment approvals. |
| **Cash Card breakage** | The breakage assumption rose from **5% to 8%** and generated a **$6.4M one-time Q4 revenue adjustment**. Mature historical cohorts showed approximately **3.7%–4.1%** still unredeemed after 36 months; these percentages are not necessarily final lifetime breakage rates. | Reperform card-level redemption analysis; test the $6.4M entry; reconcile liability activity and independently evaluate the estimate. |

### Materiality

The case specifies **$16.0M overall materiality**, **$12.0M performance materiality**, and a **$0.8M clearly trivial threshold**. Quantified exposures are *scenarios for audit planning*, not proven adjustments.

### Key assumptions and limitations

- **$644M rebate forecast:** $440M January–August purchases + four months at the $55M January–August monthly average − $16M for the announced $8M/month vendor shift in November and December. Holiday demand could make the 5% tier achievable.
- **$15.4M operating-income scenario:** If the 2% tier applies, the rebate on $440M is $8.8M; assuming 25% of related goods remain unsold, only $6.6M should be credited to current-year COGS, versus the $22M recorded. If 5% is supported, allocation of the rebate to remaining inventory still requires testing.
- **Inventory estimate:** For the 23 older-system warehouses, apply a 0.03 percentage-point shrink difference to $3,640M annual sales. For the 35 converted warehouses, apply a 0.10–0.15 percentage-point difference to $2,950M March–August sales. This is an indicative sensitivity, **not** a full-year company-wide inventory misstatement.
- **Cash Card estimate:** Unredeemed at 12 or 36 months does not, on its own, determine lifetime breakage. The $6.4M adjustment requires additional evidence before any correction is proposed.
